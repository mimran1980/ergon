cpu.max holds two numbers in µs: the quota, then the period. max means no limit. In each period the kernel lets all of the cgroup’s threads together run for at most the quota; both cases below use a 100 000 µs (100 ms) period.

How ergon-tune sees it
ThrottleWatch::check, from housekeeping every few seconds, reads cpu.stat.local first and cpu.stat only when that grew, and reports “throttled in {t} of the last {p} periods”.
The pod’s quota: a second, outer budget
The pod has a quota of its own, the sum of its containers’ limits. A container cannot see its pod’s cgroup: only cpu.stat.local (Linux 6.6 and later) shows the pod’s quota stopping it.
Fix: cpu.max = max
No budget, so the spinner never stops. Per the Kubernetes docs, kubelet 1.33+ drops the quota for a container with exclusive CPUs (static CPU manager, a Guaranteed pod, a whole-number CPU request). Never cpuCFSQuota: false: it is node-wide.

Thread mix illustrative. The spinner alone spends half the quota and is never stopped. In the second period a second spinner joins and a child process runs from 140 ms: three CPUs empty the quota at 180 ms, and the kernel stops all of them, the spinner too, until the period ends (here 20 ms; up to 100 ms). After 300 ms, in ideal arithmetic: nr_periods 3, nr_throttled 1, and throttled_usec grows by the stopped time.
