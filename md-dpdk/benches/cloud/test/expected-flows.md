## Sustained maximum over several flows at once (M msgs/s)

Several publications on host a at once, each to its own subscription on host b over a channel of its own, so on a UDP port of its own: each is a flow of its own as a cloud network counts flows (one 5-tuple). They start together and every client has an app CPU to itself; each host's one driver, its one sender and one receiver thread, carries them all. Starting together does not make their measured seconds overlap (a client started late, by an SSH retry say, has the path to itself for a while and raises the sum), so `overlap` checks it: the span every flow's measured window shares over the shortest window, each from its subscriber's wall clock (all on host b), the median across the runs counted and the range in brackets. A run whose overlap is below 0.9, or unknown (a client that reports no window, or a row from before the overlap was recorded), counts in none of `aggregate`, `per flow` and `× one flow`: `left out` counts it, each one's overlap in brackets. `aggregate` sums the flows' means over their own measured windows -- the rate the flows carried at once, which a sum of per-flow medians is not -- and `per flow` is each flow's mean. `× one flow` is the aggregate over the same mode's one-flow sustained maximum at that size in the same rep (at 32 B, the flat-out throughput run): the median of those ratios, their range in brackets, and how many reps paired where not all did. Near 1, every flow shares one ceiling (the NIC's or the instance's, or the drivers' threads'); near the number of flows, the ceiling is per flow, up to the instance's own bandwidth. `loss counters` lists every loss counter that grew on either host in any run, left out or not (`sat-` and `noport-` ones are not loss, nor is `isolated-nic-irqs`, a check on the run under the loss counters' tables; short sends are); the transport's send-side counters, whose refusals show as short sends, are listed apart.
